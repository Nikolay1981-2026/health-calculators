Teen Sleep — FAQ

How much sleep does a teenager need?

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 8–10 hours of sleep per night for teenagers aged 13–18. Most teens get only 7 hours, which is not enough.

Why do teens stay up so late?

It's biology. During puberty, the body's circadian rhythm shifts — melatonin (the sleep hormone) is released about 2 hours later than in children. This means teens naturally want to sleep later and wake later. Early school start times make this worse.

What happens if teens don't get enough sleep?

Lack of sleep in teens is linked to poor grades, mood swings, depression, weight gain, weakened immune system, and higher risk of accidents. It also affects growth and athletic performance.

Should teens use screens before bed?

Ideally, no. Blue light from screens suppresses melatonin production, making it harder to fall asleep. Encourage teens to put phones away 60 minutes before bed and read a book instead.

What can parents do to help?

Set a consistent bedtime, dim lights in the evening, keep bedrooms cool and dark, limit caffeine after 2 PM, and advocate for later school start times. Also — model good sleep habits yourself.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical advice. If your teen has chronic sleep problems, consult a pediatrician or sleep specialist.
