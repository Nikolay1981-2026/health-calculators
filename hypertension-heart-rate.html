Hypertension & Exercise — FAQ

Is it safe to exercise with high blood pressure?

Yes, for most people with controlled hypertension. Exercise actually lowers blood pressure over time. But you should aim for moderate intensity, not maximal effort. Talk to your doctor before starting a new program.

What heart rate is safe with high blood pressure?

Most people with hypertension should stay in the moderate zone (50–70% of max heart rate). Vigorous exercise (70–85%) is safe for some, but check with your doctor first. Avoid going above 85% of your max heart rate.

Which exercises are best for hypertension?

Aerobic exercise (walking, swimming, cycling) is best — 150 minutes per week of moderate activity. Strength training 2–3 times per week also helps. Avoid heavy lifting with breath-holding (Valsalva maneuver), which can spike blood pressure.

What if I take beta-blockers?

Beta-blockers lower your heart rate, so standard heart rate formulas may not apply. Your target zones will be lower. Always work with your doctor to determine safe zones if you're on beta-blockers.

When should I stop exercising?

Stop immediately if you feel chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or an irregular heartbeat. If your blood pressure is above 180/120 before exercise, skip the workout and call your doctor.
