Pregnancy Calories — FAQ

How many extra calories do I need during pregnancy?

It depends on your trimester. First trimester: no extra calories needed. Second trimester: about 340 extra calories per day. Third trimester: about 450 extra calories per day. These are averages — individual needs vary based on pre-pregnancy weight and activity.

Do I need to "eat for two"?

No. This. You only need about 340–450 extra calories per day in the second and third trimesters. That's roughly one extra snack, not double meals. Eating for two can lead to excessive weight gain.

What if I'm overweight before pregnancy?

If your pre-pregnancy BMI is 25–29.9, you may need slightly fewer extra calories. If your BMI is 30+, your doctor may recommend even less weight gain. Always follow your doctor's guidance — not just the calculator.

Can I diet during pregnancy?

No. Restrictive dieting during pregnancy is dangerous — it can harm the baby's development. Focus on eating nutrient-dense foods: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, protein, and healthy fats. If you're concerned about weight gain, talk to your doctor.

Should I take prenatal vitamins?

Yes. Prenatal vitamins are recommended for all pregnant women. They include folic acid (prevents neural tube defects), iron, calcium, and vitamin D. Take them as your doctor prescribes. Our Vitamin D Calculator can help estimate your D needs.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical advice. Always follow your OB-GYN's guidance on nutrition and weight gain during pregnancy. Individual needs vary significantly.
