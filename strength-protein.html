Protein for Strength — FAQ

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

Research shows 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight per day is optimal for muscle growth. Above 2.2 g/kg, there's little additional benefit for most people. Use the calculator above for your personalized target.

Can I eat too much protein?

For healthy people, high protein intake (up to 3 g/kg) is generally safe. But very high protein can strain the kidneys in people with existing kidney disease. If you have kidney issues, talk to your doctor.

When should I eat protein?

Spread protein evenly across 3–5 meals per day. Aim for 20–40 g per meal. The "anabolic window" (eating protein within 30 minutes after training) is less important than total daily intake — but a post-workout shake is convenient.

What are the best protein sources?

Animal sources: chicken, beef, fish, eggs, dairy, whey. Plant sources: soy, lentils, chickpeas, beans, tofu, tempeh, quinoa, hemp seeds. Plant proteins are slightly less bioavailable, so vegans may need 10–20% more protein. See our Vegan Macro Calculator.

Do I need protein supplements?

No. You can meet your protein needs with whole foods. But protein powder (whey or plant-based) is a convenient way to hit your target, especially if you struggle to eat enough protein from food.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical advice. Always consult a registered dietitian or sports nutritionist for personalized guidance, especially if you have kidney disease or other health conditions.
