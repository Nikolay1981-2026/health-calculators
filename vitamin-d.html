Vitamin D — FAQ

How much vitamin D do I need per day?

The general recommendation is 600–800 IU (15–20 mcg) per day for most adults. People over 70 need 800 IU (20 mcg). But if you live in northern latitudes or get little sun, you may need more — up to 2000 IU (50 mcg) daily.

Can I get enough vitamin D from the sun?

In summer, yes — about 15–30 minutes of midday sun on exposed skin, several times a week. But in winter, most people above 35° latitude cannot produce vitamin D from sun exposure. That's why supplements are often needed.

Who is at risk of vitamin D deficiency?

People with dark skin, older adults, those who stay indoors, people living in northern latitudes, and anyone who wears full-body coverage for religious or cultural reasons. Symptoms include fatigue, bone pain, and frequent illness.

Can I take too much vitamin D?

Yes. Vitamin D is fat-soluble, so it builds up in the body. The safe upper limit for adults is 4,000 IU (100 mcg) per day. Higher doses should only be taken under medical supervision after a blood test.

Should I test my vitamin D levels?

If you suspect deficiency, ask your doctor for a 25-hydroxyvitamin D blood test. It's the most accurate way to know your levels and adjust your dose. Don't guess — especially before taking high doses.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical advice. Always consult your doctor before starting vitamin D supplements, especially at high doses.
