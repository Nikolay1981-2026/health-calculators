Low-Carb — FAQ

How many carbs should I eat on keto?

Strict keto usually means 20–30g of net carbs per day. This keeps your body in ketosis (fat-burning mode). Some people can stay in ketosis up to 50g, but 20g is the safest starting point.

What are "net carbs"?

Net carbs = total carbs minus fiber. Fiber isn't digested and doesn't raise blood sugar, so it's excluded. Example: a food with 10g total carbs and 4g fiber has 6g net carbs.

What can I eat on a low-carb diet?

Meat, fish, eggs, cheese, non-starchy vegetables (spinach, broccoli, zucchini), nuts, seeds, avocado, olive oil, and berries in small amounts. Avoid sugar, bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, and most fruit.

Is low-carb safe for everyone?

Mostly yes, but not for everyone. People with kidney disease, pregnant women, and type 1 diabetics should be cautious and consult a doctor. For healthy adults, low-carb is safe and effective for weight loss and blood sugar control.

How do I avoid the "keto flu"?

The keto flu (fatigue, headache, irritability) happens in the first week as your body adapts. Prevent it by drinking more water and adding electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium). See our Athlete Water Calculator for hydration.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical advice. Always consult your doctor or a registered dietitian before starting a low-carb or keto diet, especially if you have diabetes or kidney disease.
