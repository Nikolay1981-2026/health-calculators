Senior BMI — FAQ

Why is BMI different for seniors?

After age 65, people naturally lose muscle mass and gain body fat. Standard BMI doesn't distinguish between muscle and fat, so a healthy-looking BMI may hide excess fat. Research shows that a slightly higher BMI (23–28) is actually protective for seniors.

What is a healthy BMI for a 70-year-old?

For adults over 65, a BMI between 23 and 28 is generally considered healthy. Below 23 is underweight (risk of frailty and falls), and above 30 is obese. This is slightly higher than the standard adult range (18.5–24.9).

Is BMI accurate for older adults?

Not perfectly. BMI doesn't measure body fat directly or distinguish muscle from fat. For seniors, waist circumference and grip strength are often better indicators of health. Use BMI as one data point, not the whole picture.

Should seniors lose weight?

It depends. If you're obese (BMI over 30) and have weight-related health issues (diabetes, joint pain), modest weight loss can help. But for many seniors, maintaining weight and muscle mass is more important than losing weight.

How can seniors maintain muscle mass?

Two things: resistance training (2–3 times per week) and adequate protein (1.0–1.2 g per kg of body weight per day). Our Strength Training Protein Calculator can help you figure out the right amount.
