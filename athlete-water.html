Athlete Water — FAQ

How much water should an athlete drink per day?

Most athletes need 2.5–3.5 liters of water per day, but this varies widely depending on body weight, workout intensity, duration, and climate. Use the calculator above for a personalized number.

Should I drink water before, during, or after exercise?

All three. Drink 500 ml about 2 hours before exercise, 150–250 ml every 15–20 minutes during exercise, and 500 ml within 30 minutes after. For workouts over 60 minutes, consider an electrolyte drink.

What are the signs of dehydration?

Dark yellow urine, thirst, dry mouth, fatigue, headache, and reduced performance. A simple check: if your urine is pale yellow, you're well hydrated. If it's dark yellow or amber, drink more water.

Can you drink too much water?

Yes — it's called hyponatremia, and it's dangerous. Drinking excessive amounts of water without electrolytes dilutes sodium in the blood. Endurance athletes are at risk. Drink to thirst and include electrolytes for long workouts.

Do I need sports drinks or just water?

For workouts under 60 minutes, water is enough. For longer or intense workouts (especially in heat), a sports drink with electrolytes and carbs helps maintain performance and prevents hyponatremia.

This calculator provides an estimate for educational purposes only. It is not medical workout advice. Always consult a sports medicine professional or registered dietitian for personalized hydration guidance.
