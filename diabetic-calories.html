Diabetic Calories — FAQ

How many calories should a diabetic eat per day?

It depends on your weight goal, activity level, and blood sugar control. Most adults with type 2 diabetes do well on 1,600–2,200 calories per day if they want to maintain weight, or 1,400–1,800 if they want to lose weight. Use the calculator above for a personalized number.

Do calories matter for blood sugar?

Yes, but carbohydrates matter more. Calories determine weight, and weight loss significantly improves blood sugar control. But the type and amount of carbs you eat has a more direct effect on blood glucose. Our calculator gives you the calorie target — pair it with a carb-conscious diet.

Is a low-calorie diet safe with diabetes?

Mostly yes, but you should work with your doctor, especially if you take insulin or sulfonylureas. Very low-calorie diets (below 1,200 for women, 1,500 for men) should be supervised. Rapid weight loss can cause hypoglycemia in people on certain medications.

What is the best diet for type 2 diabetes?

Research supports several approaches: Mediterranean, low-carb, and plant-based diets all improve blood sugar. The best one is the one you can stick with. Focus on whole foods, fiber, protein, and healthy fats — and avoid sugary drinks.

How do I count calories with diabetes?

Use a food app (MyFitnessPal, Lose It!) to log meals. Aim for consistent meal times and balanced plates: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter complex carbs. Check your blood sugar 1–2 hours after meals to learn how your body responds.

Can weight loss reverse type 2 diabetes?

In many cases, yes — especially early after diagnosis. Losing 10–15% of body weight can put type 2 diabetes into remission for some people. The DiRECT trial found that 46% of participants achieved remission with an intensive weight loss program.
